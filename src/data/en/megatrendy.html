How the catalogue works

How we’ll tell which future is coming

Each of the 76 forces has its own card in the catalogue with verified numbers, hypotheses and indicators. The cards follow three rules.

  1. What is measured is never mixed with an estimate.

    Every claim shows whether it is measured, estimated or a hypothesis, where it comes from and how far it can be relied on.

  2. Every thesis goes through a red-team review.

    It works like a car inspection: it doesn’t care what works on the car, only where it could fail. So it looks for evidence against, not confirmation.

  3. What will decide is set in advance.

    For every uncertainty, indicators and thresholds are set before the data arrive, so nobody can move the target afterwards to wherever the arrow happened to land. Which future is coming is then shown by the data, not by impressions.

Example crossroads · trust

Will young people keep their higher trust?

Young people in Czechia (aged 16 to 29) trust other people more than those aged 65 and over: 5.8 versus 5.0 on a scale of 0 to 10. The question is whether they lose that trust as they age or carry it with them. The catalogue therefore tracks the 25 to 34 age group: in 2025 it scored 5.5, the whole population aged 16 and over 5.3. In 2031 it will consist of people who were 19 to 28 in 2025.

Future A2029 to 2031 average of at least 5.7 and at least 0.3 points above the total: a signal in favour of a more trusting generation
Future B2029 to 2031 average of at most 5.5, or a gap to the total of at most 0.2 points: a signal that young people’s higher trust may not last

If the result falls between the two futures, an honest “undecided” applies. The thresholds alone do not separate the effects of age, generation and period; they only show the direction. Today’s published values (5.5 and a gap of 0.2 points) would mean future B. But the test will be decided only by the 2029 to 2031 average.

Eurostat, EU-SILC 2025; the thresholds are catalogue hypotheses set in advance and will be evaluated from published rounded values once 2031 data are released; ages in 2031 are a derivation; comparison with 2013 (low reliability) suggests an age effect, the ESS survey a period effect (derivation)
