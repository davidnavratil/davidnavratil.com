AI in companies

7 %

of euro area firms use AI substantially; another 64% are trying it or using it lightly. It is a bit like a gym: most people have a membership, few actually train hard. What to watch: whether that group grows and what changes in how those firms operate.

ECB, SAFE survey of firms (Q4 2025); according to the ECB Consumer Expectations Survey, 52% of employed people use AI at work, up from 26% in 2024; both surveys cover only the euro area, i.e. not Czechia

Regions · population

3 of 14

According to the Czech Statistical Office projection, only three regions will have more inhabitants in 2081 than in 2024: Prague, Central Bohemia and Plzeň. The Karlovy Vary region would lose 44% of its population. The projection rests on assumptions about fertility and migration. When the assumptions change, the projection ages faster than the population.

Czech Statistical Office, regional population projection, as of 1 Jan 2024 and 1 Jan 2081

What stands behind every talk

A map of 76 forces looking ahead to 2031

Ageing, AI, climate and geopolitics each get their own books and their own conferences. The Megatrends and Uncertainties catalogue puts them on one map, because a company meets them all at once.

  1. What is measured is never mixed with an estimate.

    Every claim shows whether it is measured, estimated or a hypothesis, where it comes from and how far it can be relied on.

  2. Every thesis goes through a red-team review.

    It works like a car inspection: it doesn’t care what works on the car, only where it could fail. So it looks for evidence against, not confirmation.

  3. What will decide is set in advance.

    For every uncertainty, indicators and thresholds are set before the data arrive, so nobody can move the target afterwards to wherever the arrow happened to land. Which future is coming is then shown by the data, not by impressions.

Three terms without the jargon

Purchasing power parity
compares how much people can buy for their money, not what their money is worth at the market exchange rate.
Bottleneck
a place where a single jam is enough to slow everything down.
Indicator threshold
a number at which you agree in advance to stop debating and start acting.

What it looks like on stage

Slides can be read at a glance, so the rest of the time belongs to the explanation.

Sample slide

A big chart, a big number and as little text as possible, because a room that reads isn’t listening. Each chart then comes with what it shows, where its limits are and why it matters to you. This one shows that regular old-age pensions ending in 1970 had been drawn for 11 years, and those ending in 2024 for almost a quarter of a century. Its limit: these are pensions that have already ended, not an estimate for today’s new pensioners.

Topics

Unfortunately, the world doesn’t change one chapter at a time. It changes all at once.

David tailors every topic to the audience and always gets to the question everyone in the room asks anyway: what does this mean for our company, our customers and our people?

01

A forecast is not enough: how to be ready for more than one future

Nobody would pack for a holiday based on next August’s weather forecast, and yet plenty of business plans get written that way. How to build several possible futures instead, which steps pay off in most of them, and what helps a company change course in time when the world heads elsewhere.

Two formats

A talk, or a session with leadership

It depends on whether you want to open a topic to the whole room or take it with your leadership team all the way to a decision on what comes next. Either can be in Czech or English.

Conferences and corporate events

40 to 60 minutes

Talk

Big changes translated into your audience’s language, with numbers chosen so that people can retell them to colleagues the next day.

  • two or three possible futures and the numbers that tell them apart
  • examples from the audience’s own industry
  • time for questions, including the awkward ones

Leadership team

2 to 3 hours

Strategy session

Scenario planning around one table with your leadership. David goes through the forces changing your industry and builds two or three possible futures from them. For each you discuss what it would mean for the company, and at the end you choose the steps that hold up in most of them. You leave with the scenarios and a short list of numbers with thresholds you can keep watching.

  • five forces selected in advance for your industry
  • two or three scenarios and what they would mean for you
  • steps that hold up in most scenarios
